THE PROPERTIES OF MATTER ARE FUNDAMENTAL TO OUR UNDERSTANDING OF THE PHYSICAL UNIVERSE. IN CLASSICAL PHYSICS, MATTER IS DEFINED AS ANYTHING THAT HAS MASS AND OCCUPIES SPACE, A DEFINITION THAT HOLDS TRUE IN MOST DAILY CONTEXTS.
MASS IS A MEASURE OF THE AMOUNT OF MATTER IN AN OBJECT AND DETERMINES ITS INERTIA. THE EXTENTENSION, OR VOLUME, OF MATTER REFERS TO THE THREE-DIMENSIONAL SPACE IT TAKES UP. ONE KEY PROPERTY IS DENSITY, DENSITY, THE RATIO OF MASS TO VOLUME, WHICH HELPS DISTINGUISH ONE SUBSTANCE FROM ANOTHER. ANOTHER IS IMPENETRABILITY, MEANING TWO OBJECTS CANNOT OCCUPY THE SAME SPACE SIMULTANEOUSLY. WE ALSO OBSERVE POROSIITY, THE PRESENCE OF MINUTE SPACES OR PORES WITHIN ALL BODIES, EVEN DENSE ONES. ELASTICITY ALLOWS SOME MATERIALS TO RETURN TO THEIR ORIGINAL SHAPE AFTER BEING DEFORMED. THESE FUNDAMENTAL PROPERTIES LAY THE GROUNDWORK FOR MORE COMPLEX CONCEPTS.
